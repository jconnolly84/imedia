R093 · 01 Traditional Media

Newspapers, magazines, radio and TV – why they still matter in a digital age.

← Back to iMedia Genius – R093 Revision Arcade

Traditional media includes print, radio and broadcast TV. It still reaches huge audiences and feels professional and trustworthy, even in a world full of apps and social media.

What is Traditional Media?

Traditional media refers to older, established forms of mass communication such as newspapers, magazines, radio, posters, billboards and broadcast television. These channels existed long before the internet and social media.

Messages are usually one-way: the audience receives information but cannot easily respond or interact. Traditional media is often controlled by large organisations, which can make it feel more credible and trustworthy.

Key points you must remember

  • Traditional media includes print, outdoor adverts, radio and broadcast TV.
  • It is usually one-way communication with limited interactivity.
  • It can reach very large audiences but is often expensive.
  • It is often combined with new media in modern campaigns.

The Four Traditional Sectors

Traditional media sectors grew up before the internet. They usually reach large audiences through one-way communication – but every one of them is changing fast.

Film

The film sector makes feature films, documentaries and short films, plus the products that promote them: trailers, posters and press kits.

  • Evolving: streaming premieres, digital cameras and projection, more VFX and CGI
  • Same product, many sectors: a film trailer plays in cinemas, on TV and on YouTube

Television

Television produces programmes (drama, news, soaps, reality, sport), adverts, idents and trailers.

  • Evolving: catch-up and on-demand, smart TVs, streaming services, second-screen social media
  • Broadcasters now publish clips on apps and social media as well as on air

Radio

Radio produces live shows, news bulletins, adverts, jingles and sponsorship messages.

  • Evolving: digital radio (DAB), online streams, smart speakers and podcasts
  • Shows are filmed and shared as video clips on social media

Print Publishing

Print publishing produces newspapers, magazines, books, comics and graphic novels, plus printed posters and leaflets.

  • Evolving: falling print sales, digital editions, eBooks, apps and paywalled websites
  • Many titles are now 'digital first' with a smaller print run

How Traditional Media Is Changing

The specification asks how sectors are evolving. The big idea is convergence – traditional and new media are merging.

Distribution

From scheduled to on-demand

  • Audiences watch and listen when they want
  • Streaming, catch-up and podcasts replace fixed schedules
Audience

From one-way to two-way

  • Audiences comment, share and vote on social media
  • Producers get instant feedback and data
Products

Print to digital

  • Newspapers and magazines publish online and in apps
  • Books become eBooks and audiobooks
Business

New competition

  • Competing with YouTube, streaming and social media for attention
  • Advertising money has moved online

Example: one product, many sectors

A new film's trailer is made by the film sector, but it is shown as a TV advert, played before other films in the cinema, featured in a magazine review and shared on YouTube and social media.

This is why exam answers should name the product and the sector it is being used in.

Key Terms

Media sector
A part of the media industry that makes a particular kind of product, e.g. film or radio.
Traditional media
Older media sectors that existed before the internet: film, TV, radio and print publishing.
Mass media
Media that reaches very large audiences at the same time.
One-way communication
The audience receives the message but cannot easily respond.
Broadcast
Sending content to a large audience at the same time, e.g. TV and radio.
Convergence
When different media and technologies merge, e.g. a newspaper publishing video on its app.

Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

✔ Exam tips

  • Name the sector and give a specific product example
  • Explain how a sector is evolving, with a real example (e.g. radio → podcasts)
  • Show that the same product can be used by more than one sector

✘ Common mistakes

  • Don't say traditional media is 'dead' – it still reaches millions and is evolving
  • Don't confuse sectors (e.g. television) with products (e.g. a TV advert)
  • Don't give vague answers like 'it's old' – explain the impact on the audience or producer

Types of Traditional Media

Channels that existed before the internet and social media.

  • Print: newspapers, magazines, flyers, posters, billboards.
  • Broadcast: TV and radio adverts, sponsorship idents, programme trailers.
  • Outdoor: bus stops, transport adverts, building wraps, shop signage.
  • Cinema: adverts before films, special event screenings and trailers.
  • Direct marketing: posted leaflets, catalogues and door drops.
  • Exam link: be ready to name at least three examples in 1–3 mark questions.

Strengths & Limitations

When traditional media is still powerful – and what holds it back.

  • Strength – mass reach: TV and national print can reach millions quickly.
  • Strength – perceived trust: established channels often feel more credible.
  • Limitation – low interactivity: mainly one-way communication.
  • Limitation – cost: TV, radio and large outdoor campaigns can be expensive.
  • Modern campaigns: often mix traditional with new media (social, web, apps).
  • Exam tip: say how a traditional method reaches the audience and why it suits the product.
