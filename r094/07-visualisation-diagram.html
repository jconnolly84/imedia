Visualisation Diagram

  • Create a labelled visualisation diagram of the final product.
  • Include: layout, asset placement, headings/body areas, annotations.
  • Add technical details: dimensions, DPI, file format, colour mode, fonts/colours.
  • Link annotations to the brief and target audience.
Project Log evidence:
Add screenshots and explain what you did and why.

Quick checks

  • Does this link back to the client brief?
  • Does this suit the target audience?
  • Have you taken screenshots during the process?
  • Is your work saved in a sensible, organised folder?
Common mistake:
Only showing the final outcome. Record your process and explain your decisions as you go.

Design Conventions

Layouts that work

Different products have typical layouts – posters, magazine/book covers, CD/DVD covers, leaflets, packaging, adverts and web graphics. Following conventions helps audiences understand a product instantly.

Alignment

Alignment & grids

  • Line elements up for a clean layout
White space

White space

  • Space around elements improves clarity
Colour systems

Colour systems

  • e.g. Pantone, NCS for consistent colour
House style

Visual identity

  • Apply the brand's colours, fonts and logo

Key Terms

Visualisation diagram
An annotated plan showing how a still graphic will look.
Masthead
The title of a magazine or newspaper on its cover.
White space
Empty space used to give a design clarity.
House style
Consistent design rules for a brand.

Good Practice & Watch Out For

✔ Good practice

  • Show the whole product layout
  • Include technical details (size, resolution, colour mode, format)
  • Annotate choices clearly

✘ Watch out for

  • Planning only part of the product
  • Leaving out technical properties
  • Ignoring the product's conventions
