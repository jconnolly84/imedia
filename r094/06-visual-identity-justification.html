What the Specification Says

Fitness for purpose of a visual identity

Use this checklist to make sure you can cover every part of this topic.

  • Know the purpose of visual identity: recognition/familiarity, establish a brand, develop brand loyalty, visual communication with audiences
  • Know the component features: name, logo, slogan/strap line
  • Know how elements combine to shape perception and create an emotional response
  • Know that if the impression doesn't match the desired brand identity, it is not fit for purpose
R094 · 1.1 Purpose, elements and design of visual identity

Does It Fit the Brand?

Perception and emotion

A visual identity works when its colours, shapes, typography and layout create the impression the brand wants – and appeal to its audience and market position.

Purpose

What it should achieve

  • Recognition
  • Brand loyalty
  • Clear communication
Elements

What creates the impression

  • Shape/symbol
  • Typography
  • Colour palette
  • Layout/complexity
Fit

Fit for purpose?

  • Matches brand values and positioning
  • Suits the audience and market

Key Terms

Brand identity
How a brand wants to be perceived.
Fit for purpose
Meeting the needs of the client and audience.
Perception
The impression people form of a brand.

Good Practice & Watch Out For

✔ Good practice

  • Explain why choices suit the brand and audience, not just what they are
  • Weigh up strengths and weaknesses of options
  • Use your own reasoning in your own words

✘ Watch out for

  • Describing the design instead of evaluating it
  • Ignoring brand positioning
  • Generic statements that could apply to any brand
